# Opportunity Wedge Patterns

A wedge is not a job title. It is the smallest **credible entry point** where demand, scarcity and proofability overlap.

## 1. Skill Wedge

A new capability is useful before the talent market has standardized it.

Examples:

- agent evaluation;
- context engineering;
- browser / computer-use reliability;
- multimodal retrieval evaluation;
- AI observability;
- voice-agent latency / turn-taking.

Good when:

- the candidate already has adjacent technical fundamentals;
- the new skill can be demonstrated in 2–14 days;
- the skill appears in real tasks even if not in job titles.

Bad when:

- it is already a standard checkbox on most resumes;
- the skill is easy to fake with a tutorial demo.

---

## 2. Problem Wedge

Enter through a painful problem rather than a named skill.

Examples:

- tool-calling failures;
- hallucination in high-stakes workflow;
- agent regression testing;
- inference cost explosion;
- onboarding friction for an AI developer tool;
- data quality for a vertical AI product.

Good when:

- multiple teams independently report the same pain;
- solving the problem naturally demonstrates several skills;
- the problem has a measurable before / after.

---

## 3. Distribution Wedge

The scarce asset is a combination of technical understanding and access to a market / audience / domain.

Examples:

- AI developer + Chinese developer community distribution;
- technical founder tooling + creator growth;
- AI + education operations;
- AI + cross-border GTM;
- quant + engineering + content explanation.

Good when:

- the user has genuine distribution or domain credibility;
- competitors often have only one side of the combination.

Do not invent “domain expertise” from superficial exposure.

---

## 4. Evidence Wedge

The market already has many candidates, but few can show hard evidence.

Examples:

- benchmarked instead of demoed;
- deployed instead of prototyped;
- merged PR instead of “open-source enthusiast”;
- measured cost / latency instead of “optimized performance”;
- real-user feedback instead of hypothetical personas.

This is often the fastest wedge for students and career switchers.

---

## 5. Organization Wedge

A role is not rare globally, but a specific company stage creates unusual demand.

Examples:

- early startup needs one engineer who can build + talk to users;
- research team needs a product-minded benchmark builder;
- international expansion needs bilingual technical operator;
- new product line needs internal tooling before formal hiring catches up.

The wedge is tied to **who values the evidence**, not just what the evidence is.

---

## 6. Contribution Wedge

Instead of self-building everything, contribute to a real ecosystem where the target team already pays attention.

Examples:

- docs / examples / eval tasks in a relevant open-source project;
- issue reproduction and fix;
- benchmark contribution;
- integration adapter;
- developer experience improvement.

Use `/contributor` when external validation is more valuable than another private project.

---

## 7. Wedge combinations

The strongest wedge is often a combination:

```text
Problem Wedge + Evidence Wedge
Agent reliability pain + reproducible benchmark
```

```text
Skill Wedge + Distribution Wedge
AI automation + education operations
```

```text
Contribution Wedge + Organization Wedge
Open-source contribution to a project used by a target startup
```

Do not maximize novelty for its own sake. Prefer the smallest wedge that can create **credible career leverage**.
